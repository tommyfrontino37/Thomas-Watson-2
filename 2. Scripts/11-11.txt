Men drink the poison of sin in a merriment, but it costs them their souls: ‘the wages of sin is death’ (Romans, chapter 6, verse 23). What Solomon said of wine may also be said of sin: at first ‘it giveth his colour in the cup. At the last it biteth like a serpent, and stingeth like an adder’ (Proverbs, chapter 23, verses 31 and 32). Christ tell us of the worm and the fire (Mark, chapter 9, verse 48). Sin is like oil, and God’s wrath is like fire. As long as the damned continue sinning, so the fire will continue scorching, and ‘who among us shall dwell with everlasting burnings?’ (Isa. 33.14). But men question the truth of this and are like impious Devonax who, being threatened with hell for his villainies, mocked at it and said, I will believe there is a hell when I come there, and not before. We cannot make hell enter into men till they enter into hell. Thus we have seen the deadly evil in sin which, seriously considered, may make us repent and turn to God. If, for all this, men will persist in sin and are resolved upon a voyage to hell, who can help it? They have been told what a soul-damning rock sin is, but if they will voluntarily run upon it and split themselves, their blood be upon their own head. 2. The second serious consideration to work repentance is to consider the mercies of God.
