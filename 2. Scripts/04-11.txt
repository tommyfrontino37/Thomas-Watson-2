5. Where there is a real hatred, we not only oppose sin in ourselves but in others too. The church at Ephesus could not bear with those who were evil (Revelation, chapter 2, verse 2). Paul sharply censured Peter for his dissimulation although he was an apostle. Christ in a holy displeasure whipped the money-changers out of the temple (John, chapter 2, verse 15). He would not suffer the temple to be made an exchange. Nehemiah rebuked the nobles for their usury (Nehemiah, chapter 3, verse 7) and their Sabbath profanation (Nehemiah, chapter 13, verse 17). A sin-hater will not endure wickedness in his family: ‘He that worketh deceit shall not dwell within my house’ (Psalm 101, verse 7). What a shame it is when magistrates can show height of spirit in their passions but no heroic spirit in suppressing vice. Those who have no antipathy against sin are strangers to repentance. Sin is in them as poison in a serpent, which, being natural to it, affords delight. How far are they from repentance who, instead of hating sin, love sin! To the godly sin is as a thorn in the eye; to the wicked it is as a crown on the head: ‘When thou doest evil, then thou rejoicest’ (Jeremiah, chapter 11, verse 13). Loving of sin is worse than committing it. A good man may run into a sinful action unawares, but to love sin is desperate. What is it that makes a swine but loving to tumble in the mire? What is it that makes a devil but loving that which opposes God?
