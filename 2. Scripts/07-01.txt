Chapter Seven. A Serious Exhortation to Repentance. That the exhortation to repentance may be more quickened, I shall lay down some powerful motives to excite repentance. 1. Sorrow and melting of heart fits us for every holy duty. A piece of lead, while it is in the lump, can be put to no use, but melt it, and you may then cast it into any mould, and it is made useful. So a heart that is hardened into a lump of sin is good for nothing, but when it is dissolved by repentance, it is useful. A melting heart is fit to pray. When Paul’s heart was humbled and melted, then ‘behold, he prayeth’ (Acts, chapter 9, verse 11). It is fit to hear the word. Now the word works kindly. When Josiah’s heart was tender, he humbled himself and rent his clothes at the hearing of the words of the law (Second Chronicles, chapter 34, verse 19). His heart, like melting wax, was ready to take any seal of the word. A melting heart is fit to obey. When the heart is like metal in the furnace, it is facile and malleable to anything: ‘Lord, what wilt thou have me to do?’ (Acts, chapter 9, verse 6). A repenting soul subscribes to God’s will and answers to his call, as the echo to the voice. 2. Repentance is highly acceptable. When a spiritual river runs to water this garden, then our hearts are a garden of Eden, delightful to God. I have read that doves delight to be about the waters. And surely God’s Spirit, who descended in the likeness of a dove, takes great delight in the waters of repentance.
