(6) Sin, like Circe’s enchanting cup, turns men into beasts (Psalm 49, verse 12), and is not that matter for shame? Sinners are compared to foxes (Luke, chapter 13, verse 32), to wolves (Matthew, chapter 7, verse 15), to asses (Job, chapter 11, verse 12), to swine (Second Peter, chapter 2, verse 22). A sinner is a swine with a man’s head. He who was once little less than the angels in dignity is now become like the beasts. Grace in this life does not wholly obliterate this brutish temper. Agur, that good man, cried out, ‘Surely I am more brutish than any!’ (Proverbs, chapter 30, verse 2). But common sinners are in a manner wholly brutified; they do not act rationally but are carried away by the violence of their lusts and passions. How may this make us ashamed who are thus degenerated below our own species? Our sins have taken away that noble, masculine spirit which once we had. The crown is fallen from our head. God’s image is defaced, reason is eclipsed, conscience stupified. We have more in us of the brute than of the angel. (7) In every sin there is folly (Jeremiah, chapter 4, verse 22). A man will be ashamed of his folly. Is not he a fool who labours more for the bread that perishes than for the bread of life? Is not he a fool who for a lust or a trifle will lose heaven, like Tiberius who for a draught of drink forfeited his kingdom? Is not he a fool who, to safeguard his body, will injure his soul? As if one should let his arm or head be cut to save his vest!
